Module 1: Industry Essentials & Key Terms

            Day 1 • Slide 2 of 9         
        
            
                

Overview: Master the core trucking vocabulary, the four main industry players, and the strict legal line between dispatching and brokering.

                                 

Core Vocabulary – Definitions:

                
                        
  • FMCSA – Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration. The U.S. government agency that regulates the trucking industry, safety, and operating authority.
    •                     
  • DOT Number – A unique identification number issued by the FMCSA to every commercial motor carrier that operates in interstate commerce.
    •                     
  • MC Number – Motor Carrier Number. Issued by the FMCSA to carriers and brokers that have operating authority to haul regulated commodities for hire.
    •                     
  • MOTUS – A real-time motor carrier monitoring system used by many brokers and dispatchers to check authority status, insurance, and safety data.
    •                     
  • Load Board – An online marketplace (DAT, Truckstop, Trucker Path, etc.) where brokers post available freight and carriers/dispatchers search for loads.
    •                     
  • Capacity – Available trucks/trailers that are empty and ready to haul freight.
    •                     
  • Deadhead – Miles driven with an empty trailer (no paying load). Also called “empty miles.”
    •                     
  • Backhaul – A return load that takes the truck back toward its home base or next desired region, reducing deadhead.
    •

Key Players & The Legal Line

            Day 1 • Slide 3 of 9         
        
            
                

Roles & Relationships – Clear Definitions:

                
                        
  • Shipper / Receiver – The company that owns the freight (shipper) or the company that receives it (receiver). They pay the broker or carrier.
    •                     
  • Carrier – The trucking company (owner-operator or fleet) that actually moves the freight with its own authority (MC/DOT numbers) and equipment.
    •                     
  • Freight Broker – A company that arranges transportation between shippers and carriers. Brokers never own the trucks. They must hold a $75,000 surety bond and FMCSA broker authority.
    •                     
  • Freight Dispatcher – An independent agent who works exclusively for the carrier under a written agreement. The dispatcher finds loads, negotiates rates, and handles paperwork on behalf of the carrier. The dispatcher never takes possession of the freight or the money paid for the load.
    •                 
                

Critical Legal Rule: If you ever handle the load money or act as a middleman between shipper and carrier without a written carrier agreement, you are brokering and must have a broker bond. Stay strictly on the dispatcher side of the line.

            
            
                                 Shipper → Broker → Carrier (Dispatcher works only for the Carrier)

Module 2: Business Structure, Tech Stack & Niche

            Day 1 • Slide 5 of 9         
        
            
                

Overview: Form your LLC, set up essential tech/cloud systems, and choose a profitable equipment niche.

                
                        
  • LLC (Limited Liability Company) – A legal business structure that protects your personal assets from business debts and lawsuits.
    •                     
  • EIN (Employer Identification Number) – A free nine-digit tax ID number issued by the IRS. Required to open a business bank account and file taxes as a business.
    •                     
  • Merchant Processing – Services such as Square or Stripe that allow you to accept credit cards and send automated invoices to your carriers.
    •                     
  • VoIP Phone – Voice over Internet Protocol. A business phone system that runs over the internet (OpenPhone, Google Voice, RingCentral) instead of a traditional landline.
    •                     
  • E-Signature Software – Tools such as DocuSign or PandaDoc that let carriers sign contracts and Ratecons electronically.
    •                     
  • Niche – A focused segment of the market (Box Trucks, Dry Vans, Reefers, Flatbeds, specific geographic lanes, or new-authority carriers) that you specialize in to stand out and win higher rates.
    •

Visual Walkthrough — Drive Structure & Carrier Packet

Live / screenshot preview of:

  • Dispatch Agreement – The contract between you and the carrier that outlines your services, fee percentage, and responsibilities.

    •                 
                

These documents become the backbone of every carrier relationship. Keep them clean, consistent, and always signed before the first load.
